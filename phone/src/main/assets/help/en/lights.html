Screen help

Lights

Pick the light that fits the moment. Two of the lights fill the whole screen and have no help button of their own, so this page tells you how to leave each one before you open it.

In this order

  1. Choose the light: 1 for rain, a river or wet hands, 2 to read a map or look for something in a bag, 3 for the plain beam of the camera flash.
  2. Before you open 1, remember its exit: the screen ignores every touch, and a volume key leaves it.
  3. Before you open 2, remember that a tap on the screen outside its controls closes it; drag up or down to change the brightness.
  4. Tap 3 to switch the camera flash on and tap it again to switch it off; no screen opens.
  5. Switch on 4 to keep the camera flash one tap away in the notification shade.

The elements

1Water flashlightA white lamp whose screen ignores every touch.

Android first asks you to pin the app. Confirm before using the light with wet hands; if you dismiss the system request, choose Start to try again or Cancel to return. Once pinned, the camera flash and the white screen light together, and the time and hint Locked. Use the buttons appear. Touches and BACK do not close the lamp.

Why
Use it in rain, at a river or with wet hands, where drops on the glass would otherwise press buttons; the home-screen widget Water flashlight opens it in one tap.
Next
The light stays on and the screen stays awake until you leave it.
Undo
Press a volume key: the light goes out and you are back where you came from.
Shortcut
Volume up, Volume down, or the mute, camera, headset or play/pause key if the phone has one.
Tip

This is a touch lock, not waterproofing: it does not protect the phone from water. Pinning blocks repeated Home and edge-back swipes. Android keeps its own emergency unpin escape: with gesture navigation, swipe up and hold; with buttons, follow Android's pinning instructions. If you leave the lamp through the system, the flash goes out. The power button turns the screen and flash off; a volume key unpins and closes the lamp. No help button or F1 action interrupts this screen.

2Front flashlightThe screen as a lamp, in a colour you choose.

A tap on the screen outside its controls closes it. Until then the whole screen glows and shows the time: drag up or down, or tap one of the five brightness steps, to change the brightness, and the Light color button changes the colour.

Why
Use it to read a map or look for something in a bag; the home-screen widget Front Flashlight opens it in one tap.
Next
The screen stays awake while it glows; the light starts at full brightness each time and remembers the colour.
Undo
Tap the screen outside the controls, or press Back: the light closes and you are back where you came from.
Shortcut
Enter on a keyboard, or the centre key of a D-pad, when no control has the focus.
Tip

This screen has no help button and F1 does nothing here: a help control would be one more way to close the light. Only the window's brightness changes, never the phone's own brightness setting.

3Camera flashlightThe camera flash as a plain torch.

Switches the camera flash on or off at once and opens no screen of its own. If the flash cannot be switched, a short message says so.

Why
Use it when a plain beam is enough and you want to keep using the phone.
Next
The flash stays lit until you switch it off; the row shows no state of its own.
Undo
Tap the row again to switch the flash off.
4Flashlight in the notification shadeThe camera flash from the notification shade.

A checkbox, shown only on a phone with a camera flash. When it is on, a permanent entry Camera flashlight waits in the notification shade, and a tap on it lights or puts out the flash without opening the app.

Why
Use it to reach the torch from any screen.
Next
On Android 13 and later the app first asks for permission to show notifications, and the checkbox stays off until you allow it.
Undo
Switch it off and the entry leaves the shade.
Tip

If notifications are blocked for the app in the phone settings, the entry does not appear. After the phone restarts the entry comes back by itself.

5Help (F1)Opens this page.

The question mark in the top bar opens the page for the list of lights. The two full-screen lights do not have this button.

Why
It explains the screen you are looking at, with a picture, without leaving the app and without a network.
Shortcut
F1 on a connected keyboard does the same.
