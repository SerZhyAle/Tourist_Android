Tourist.

Privacy Policy for Tourist

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Tourist is a hike kit for an Android phone and a Wear OS watch: a dashboard with heading, speed, altitude, coordinates, satellites, sunrise and sunset, steps and trip distance; an SOS signal; a water flashlight that no wet touch can switch off; the screen and camera flashlights; and a multi-participant stopwatch. It is published by Serhii Zhyhunenko (SerZhyAle) as three packages on Google Play: the phone app and the Wear OS app, which share the identifier com.sza.tourist, and the watch face com.sza.tourist.watchface. This policy covers all three and says which one it means where they differ.

In short:

  • The developer receives none of your data. The developer runs no server, keeps no accounts, and the apps contain no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking code.
  • One thing leaves the phone on its own: the weather request. To show the weather, sunrise and sunset, the phone app sends the coordinates of your current position, or of a place you searched for, to the free weather service Open-Meteo. Section 4 says exactly what is sent.
  • Everything else stays on your devices, unless you share it, save it, or start SOS, which reaches your own paired watch or phone.

1. What the apps do not do

  • They have no telemetry: no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no advertising identifier and no third-party tracking library. The components they are built from are listed in section 8.
  • They create no account and ask you to sign in nowhere.
  • They read your location only while a Tourist screen is open on the phone, never in the background, and they declare no background location permission. The watch app and the watch face do not ask for your location.
  • The camera permission switches the camera's flash on and off as a flashlight. The apps take no pictures, record no video and record no sound.
  • SOS sounds and blinks on the device and reaches your paired watch or phone. It sends no SMS, makes no call and contacts no emergency service.
  • They do not read your contacts, photos, messages or the data of other apps.

2. What the apps read on your device

  • Location (phone): while the dashboard or another Tourist screen is open, the phone app reads the position, speed, altitude and satellite status from Android's own location service, for the dashboard tiles, the trip distance and the weather.
  • Sensors: the compass, barometer, gyroscope and step sensors of the device, for the heading, the pressure, the 3D phone tile, and the steps and cadence tiles. These readings never leave the device.
  • Audio files (phone): when you choose a track for the stopwatch, the app lists the audio files on the phone so you can pick one. The track is played on the phone and is not copied anywhere.

3. What the apps store

  • Phone: your settings (theme, language, units, whether instrument screens stay awake), the counter, the current trip summary (distance, elevation gain and loss, top speed, time in motion), the stopwatch and widget state, which permission questions you have answered, and the last weather received for each place. The weather is kept under the place's coordinates rounded to two decimals, about one kilometre.
  • Watch: its settings, the counter and the stopwatch state. The watch face stores nothing.
  • Android backup is switched off for all three packages, so none of this is copied to your Google account. Everything is removed when you uninstall the app.

4. What leaves the device

On its own, the phone app sends only these weather requests, over an encrypted HTTPS connection:

  • Weather. The latitude and longitude of your current position, or of the place you picked, with the temperature unit and the time-zone setting, go to api.open-meteo.com. The answer is the current weather and today's sunrise and sunset for that place.
  • Place search. When you search for a place for the weather, the text you type and the app's language go to geocoding-api.open-meteo.com, which answers with matching places.

The requests carry no account, name, device identifier or advertising identifier. Open-Meteo, like any internet service, sees the network address the request comes from. Open-Meteo is an independent service with its own terms and privacy statement (open-meteo.com/en/terms); the developer receives nothing from it. With the location permission denied, the phone sends no position.

Everything else leaves a device only through an action you choose:

  • Map opens your maps app at your coordinates. Share hands the text "Coordinates:" with your latitude and longitude and a maps link to the app you pick in the Android share sheet. Tourist does not choose the destination and does not see it.
  • SOS started on the phone starts on your paired watch, and the other way round. The start and stop commands travel over the Wearable Data Layer of Google Play services, a link between your own devices.
  • Watch and phone also exchange, over the same link, the settings the two apps share. Nothing goes through a server of the developer.
  • Diagnostic file: Settings, About can write a ZIP file of build and platform values to a place you choose in the Android file picker. It contains no location, no history, no music paths and no text you typed, and the app never uploads it.

5. Permissions

There is nothing else. The watch app has no location, internet, body-sensor or activity permission, and the watch face has no permission and no code of its own.

6. Google Play services and Google Play

The link between watch and phone uses the Wearable Data Layer of Google Play services, which Google runs on the devices under its own terms. The phone reads your location through Android itself, not through Google Play services. Google Play, the store, may give the developer aggregated statistics such as installs and crash rates from devices whose owners share them with Google. The developer receives no device-level data from the apps.

7. Children

The apps are not directed at children under 13, and the developer collects personal data from no one.

8. Third-party components

The apps are built from these open-source components: AndroidX, Material Components, Jetpack Compose and Wear Compose, Accompanist, Dagger and Hilt, OkHttp, Gson, Timber, the Kotlin libraries and Google Play services (Wearable). None of them is an analytics, advertising or tracking library.

9. Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it stores. You can also clear its storage in the Android settings. A diagnostic file you saved, or text you shared, is yours to delete. The developer holds no copy of anything.

10. Open source

Tourist is open source software released under the MIT License. The full source code is publicly available at GitHub.

11. Changes to this policy

When the apps change what they read, store or send, this page changes in the same release and the "Last updated" date at the top moves. The text on this page is the text of the version of the apps that is currently published.
